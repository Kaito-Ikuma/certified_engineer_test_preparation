This program was developed to do rapid computation of theoretical Schlumberger
resistivity sounding curves. It is based on the application of Ghosh's linear
filter (Ghosh, 1971) to resistivity transforms calculated for a given earth model
Haines and Campbell (1980) wrote a program for the computation of Schlumberger
and Wenner sounding curves over structures with as many as 10 horizontal layers.
Although it is generally not possible to visually recognize more than 8 layers
on a field curve, the present program can be used to compute sounding curves
from digitized electric logs provided the number of layers does not exceed 25.
The input data consists of the thicknesses, the resistivities, and the number
of layers (up to 25). The program will compute the apparent resistivities at
the rate of 6 points per logarithmic cycle starting at abscissa value of AB/2
of 1 and ending at 10,000. The computed apparent resistivities will be stored
successively in registers 37 to 61. This represents the output of the program,
and the contents of these registers can be recalled by the user.
Note:   The dimensions of the computed electrode spacings (AB/2) and apparent
        resistivities will correspond to those of the entered thicknesses and
        resistivities, respectively.
